Iberia · PT

THE PORTUGAL NOBODY TELLS YOU ABOUT

Everyone lands in Lisbon and drives south. The part worth your week is the 300km you went past on the way.

Hidden gems

4 places

Monsanto

town

A village built in and around granite boulders the size of houses — roofs wedged under them, walls running into them, one boulder serving as somebody's ceiling. There's a ruined castle at the top and a very long view into Spain.

Nearest town — Castelo Branco 40.040 N · 7.110 W

Getting there. About 50 minutes from Castelo Branco by car. The village road is single track and steep; leave the car in the lower car park.

Peneda-Gerês

mountain

The only national park in the country, up against the Spanish border. Granite, wild ponies, and villages with communal grain stores still standing on stilts. The Arado waterfall is the one everyone finds; walk twenty minutes upstream and you get it to yourself.

Nearest town — Arcos de Valdevez 41.730 N · 8.150 W

Getting there. Two hours from Porto by car. Buses reach Arcos de Valdevez and then stop being useful — you need your own wheels for the park roads.

Piódão

town

A schist village stacked up one side of a valley in the Serra do Açor, every house the same dark slate, every door and window frame painted blue. It was cut off by road until the 1970s, which is why it still looks like this.

Nearest town — Arganil 40.230 N · 7.830 W

Getting there. Ninety minutes from Coimbra on mountain roads that take longer than the map says. No bus worth the name. Park at the top and walk down.

Praia da Ursa

coast

Sea stacks and black sand under the cliffs just north of Cabo da Roca, the westernmost point of mainland Europe. The descent is a steep unmade path with a rope in one section, which is the only reason it isn't rammed.

Nearest town — Colares 38.790 N · 9.480 W

Getting there. Drive to Cabo da Roca from Sintra, then the unmarked pull-in about 1km north. Twenty minutes down, thirty-five back up. Not at high tide, and not in the dark.

The obvious ones

Lisbon
Worth the crowds. Ride tram 28 before eight or don't bother.
Porto
Two days minimum. Cross the Dom Luís I bridge on the upper deck, walk back on the lower.
Sintra
Pena Palace is as good as people say and as busy as people warn. First entry slot or skip it.
Douro Valley
Terraced vineyards on both banks. The train from Porto runs the whole way along the river.
The Algarve
Genuinely beautiful. In August it is a car park with a beach attached. Go in May.

When to go

Spring

March to May is the best window. Alentejo is green, the sea is still cold, and rooms cost half what they will in July.

Summer

July and August are hot and full. The interior passes 40°C most weeks. North of Porto the coast stays usable.

Autumn

September and October. Harvest in the Douro, warmest sea of the year, and the coach parties have gone.

Winter

Wet in the north, bright and mild in the south. Some interior guesthouses shut from November to February — ring ahead.

Getting around

Trains, boats, and whether you need a car

Trains connect Lisbon, Coimbra and Porto properly — book the Alfa Pendular a day ahead and it's cheap. East of that line you need a car; buses to the interior villages run twice a day at best. The Douro line from Porto to Pocinho is worth a day on its own, and you can put a bike on it.
